About Drift

Built to answer
four questions.

The NASA Space Apps 2026 challenge "Be an Earth System Trend Detective" asks for a tool that shows what is changing, where, by how much, and whether the change is significant. Drift does all four for every grid cell on Earth, live in the browser.

How it meets the challenge

Every requirement in the brief, and where to find it in Drift.

The brief asksDrift's answer
Select variablesNASA GISTEMP v4 surface temperature and GPCP v2.3 precipitation on the map, plus humidity and wind from NASA POWER (MERRA-2) in the Point lab. Any season, any window of years.
Acquire data for a regionClick a cell, drag a box, or pick a detected hotspot. Regional series use area weights and skip years with poor coverage.
Visualise change over timeGlobe, Equal Earth map, year-by-year animation, climate stripes, time series with fitted trend and 95% band.
What, where, how muchA written answer generated from the result, the share of Earth's surface that changed, Sen's slope per decade and total change.
SignificanceMann–Kendall test corrected for persistence (Hamed–Rao), confidence intervals, and false discovery control across the whole map.
Opposite regional trendsConnected regions of robust change in both directions, plus a latitude profile.
Help for studentsA guided tour, six detective case files, a step-by-step method explainer and a plain-language Field Guide.

How it works

From NASA's original files to an answer on your screen. Nothing is interpolated, filled in or pre-written.

Data and credits

NASA data

GISTEMP v4

NASA Goddard Institute for Space Studies surface temperature analysis, 2° grid, anomalies vs 1951–1980. data.giss.nasa.gov/gistemp

NASA data

GPCP v2.3

Global Precipitation Climatology Project monthly analysis (NASA GSFC / UMD), 2.5° grid, distributed by NOAA NCEI. Adler et al., 2018.

NASA data

NASA POWER (MERRA-2)

Monthly point records of temperature, rainfall, humidity and wind from NASA Langley's POWER service, built on NASA GMAO's MERRA-2 reanalysis. Used in the Point lab. power.larc.nasa.gov
